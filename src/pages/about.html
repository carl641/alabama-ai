How we work

The principles behind every project

These are the rules we build by. They shape what we'll recommend, what we'll refuse to do, and how a project is handed over.

Start with the workflow, not the tool

We begin by understanding how the work happens today: who does it, how often, what goes wrong. The technology choice comes after that, and sometimes the answer is a form or a feature you already pay for.

Measure before and after

Every build starts with a baseline of time, volume or errors, so improvement can be checked rather than assumed. If we can't define how success will be measured, the project isn't ready to start.

People approve what matters

Anything that commits your business to a price, a promise, a payment or a sensitive reply goes to a person first. AI drafts and routes; your team decides.

Small pilots before big builds

We prefer a narrow, tested pilot over a sprawling first project. It's cheaper to learn from, easier to evaluate and simpler to stop if it isn't working.

You own it

Accounts, data, prompts, configurations and documentation belong to you. Wherever practical, systems run in accounts in your business's name. See how ownership works.

Say what we don't know

If a tool has limits, a workflow is a poor fit for AI, or a question needs a lawyer or accountant, we'll say so plainly instead of guessing.

See the work

Judge us on what we build

Our working demonstrations show how we approach a real problem: the steps, the handoffs to people and the limits. They use made-up sample data and are examples of our build approach, not results from client projects.
