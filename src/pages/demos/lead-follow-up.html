The scenario

What happens to a web lead, step by step

Most small businesses lose leads in the gap between "form submitted" and "someone replied." This workflow closes that gap without letting software speak for the business unsupervised.

  1. Check

    Required fields and email format are validated, and the email is matched against existing contacts so a repeat inquiry updates the same record instead of creating a duplicate.

  2. Classify

    Transparent rules sort the lead by intent (repair, replacement estimate, maintenance) and urgency, and flag safety words that need a person immediately.

  3. Log & route

    A CRM record is created and assigned to a role, such as the comfort advisor for estimates or the dispatcher for repairs, so ownership is never unclear.

  4. Draft

    A reply is filled in from an approved template, using the lead's name, city and request. It never includes prices or promises.

  5. Approve

    The owner reviews, edits if needed and approves. Only then is the reply sent and a follow-up task scheduled.

Safeguards

Built so it can't embarrass you

  • Human approval before sending. In this demo every reply waits for a person. In a real build, you decide which (if any) low-risk replies could ever skip review, and that decision is written down.
  • No automatic pricing or commitments. Templates never quote prices, discounts, warranties or arrival times. If an edited draft mentions a price or a guarantee, the demo flags it for sign-off.
  • Safety words go to a person. Messages mentioning gas, smoke or carbon monoxide are routed to on-call staff with a safety-first reply, not a sales follow-up.
  • Opt-out respected. Every reply includes a way to opt out, and an opt-out cancels the scheduled follow-ups.
  • A named owner for every lead. Routing assigns a role, so there's always one person responsible for the next step.
Limits

What this demo doesn't show

  • Real integrations. The CRM, email and task list here are simulated. A real build connects to the tools you already use.
  • Language models on messy input. The demo uses simple keyword rules so you can see exactly why a lead was classified the way it was. Production systems may use an AI model for classification and drafting, which then needs testing against your real inquiries.
  • Performance. There are no response-time, conversion or accuracy figures here, because a demo can't produce honest ones.
  • Deliverability and compliance. Sending real email and text messages involves sender setup, consent and opt-out rules that are reviewed per project.
Building a real one

What a production version needs

To turn this into a working system for your business, we'd need to agree on where leads come from (web forms, email, ads, phone messages), which CRM or spreadsheet is the record, who owns each type of lead, and which reply templates you're comfortable with. Then we build, test with sample leads, run it alongside your current process, and only switch over once your team trusts it.
