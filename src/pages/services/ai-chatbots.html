What a business chatbot is, and isn't

A well-built chatbot is a question-answering system tied to a defined set of knowledge sources. It isn't a general AI assistant let loose on your website. We decide together what it may answer, what it must refuse, and when it hands over to a person.

Where accuracy matters, the chatbot shows which document or page its answer came from, so customers and staff can check it.

Good fit

  • The same questions arrive by chat, email or phone every week
  • The answers already exist in documents, web pages or policies
  • Staff lose time looking up internal procedures
  • Someone can own keeping the source content up to date

Probably not a fit

  • The answers aren't written down anywhere yet
  • Questions need account-specific decisions a person must make
  • You need legal, medical or financial advice given to customers
  • Most inquiries arrive by phone: consider an AI receptionist
Use cases

Where chatbots earn their keep

Website questions

Service area, hours, what's included, how to prepare, what happens next. Answers visitors at any hour and collects details when they're ready to buy.

Lead capture

When a visitor is ready, the chatbot gathers the basics and passes a tidy summary to your team or CRM. Pairs well with workflow automation.

Internal knowledge

Staff ask "how do we handle a return?" or "what's the policy on overtime?" and get the answer from your handbook, with a link to the section.

New-hire support

Answers routine onboarding questions so managers aren't interrupted for the same things every time someone starts.

Product & service details

Specifications, options and comparisons drawn from your own catalog or service sheets.

After-hours triage

Sorts overnight inquiries by type and urgency so your team starts the day knowing what matters.

Testing & failure handling

How we keep answers accurate

  • Answers come only from approved sources; questions outside scope get a polite refusal and a handoff
  • We test against an evaluation set before launch and re-run it after every content or model change
  • Conversations are reviewed during the first weeks to catch wrong or weak answers
  • Clear disclosure that visitors are talking to an AI, with an easy way to reach a person
  • No chatbot is error-free. We design for catching mistakes, not promising there won't be any

Questions about AI chatbots

Will the chatbot make things up?

AI models can produce confident wrong answers. We reduce that risk by limiting answers to your approved content, showing sources, refusing out-of-scope questions and testing against real questions. We can't promise zero errors, so handoff to a person is always available.

How is this different from the chat widget we already have?

Many existing widgets are live chat (a person answers) or scripted menus. An AI chatbot understands free-text questions and answers from your content. Sometimes the right move is adding AI to the tool you already have. We'll check that first.

Who keeps the content up to date?

Your team, with an update guide from us, or us under a support arrangement. Someone must own it: an out-of-date chatbot gives out-of-date answers.

Can it see customer accounts or orders?

It can be connected to other systems, but that raises the stakes on access and privacy. We usually start with public or internal reference content and add account lookups only when there's a clear need. See our security approach.
