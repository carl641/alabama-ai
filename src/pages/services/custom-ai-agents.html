Custom AI Agent Development

An AI agent is software that works through a multi-step task on its own: it reads, decides what to do next and takes actions in your systems. We build agents for bounded jobs, with the minimum permissions they need and a person approving anything with consequences.

When you need an agent, and when you don't

If a task always follows the same steps, a standard workflow automation is cheaper, faster and more predictable. An agent earns its extra cost when the steps depend on what it finds: which records to check, which rule applies, what to do when information conflicts.

We'll recommend the simpler option whenever it will do the job. More on that choice in AI vs. traditional automation.

Good fit

  • A task that needs reading and judgment across two or more systems
  • Steps vary case by case, but the goal and the limits are clear
  • A person can check the result faster than doing the work
  • You're willing to start narrow and expand only on evidence

Probably not a fit

  • The steps are always the same: use workflow automation instead
  • Mistakes would be costly and can't be caught before they happen
  • The task has no clear definition of "done"
  • You want a general-purpose AI employee with access to everything
Use cases

Bounded tasks agents handle well

Illustrative examples. Every agent we build is scoped to one clearly defined job.

Research & preparation

Gather information on a new lead or job from your records and approved sources, and prepare a briefing before the first call.

Reconciliation

Compare records across two systems, such as job logs against invoices, and list mismatches with a suggested fix for a person to confirm.

Request handling

Read an incoming request, look up the relevant account or policy, and prepare the response or next action for approval.

Scheduling coordination

Work out who is available and qualified, propose assignments and flag conflicts for a dispatcher.

Document review

Check incoming documents against a checklist and report what's missing or inconsistent.

Internal assistant

Answer staff questions that need looking up live data, not just documents, within the permissions that staff member already has.

Controls

How we keep agents in bounds

The risk with agents isn't that they're unintelligent. It's that they can act. Our designs limit what they're able to do.

Bounded task

A written definition of the agent's job, its inputs, its expected outputs and what counts as out of scope. Anything outside it is refused or handed to a person.

Least-privilege tool access

The agent gets its own credentials with only the permissions its task needs. Read-only wherever possible; no access to systems it doesn't use.

Approval gates

Actions that send messages, spend money, change records or make commitments wait for a person to approve, until you decide otherwise based on evidence.

Full activity log

Every step the agent takes and the reason it gave are recorded, so you can review what happened and why.

Limits & stop switch

Caps on how many actions it can take and how much it can spend on AI usage, plus a simple way to pause it.

Your ownership

Code, prompts, configuration and documentation belong to you, in accounts you control wherever possible.

Testing & failure handling

Evaluation before trust

Before an agent touches live systems, we test it against a set of cases drawn from your real work (with sensitive details removed), including awkward ones: missing information, conflicting records, requests that look legitimate but shouldn't be acted on.

  • We measure how often it completes the task correctly, and how it fails when it doesn't
  • It runs in a "suggest only" mode first; people compare its output with their own
  • When a tool or AI service is unavailable, it stops and reports rather than improvising
  • Model or prompt changes are re-tested against the full evaluation suite before release

Scope & cost

How agent projects are priced

Custom agents usually cost more than standard automations because they need more design, testing and monitoring. The main factors are the number of tools the agent uses, how varied the cases are, and how strict the approval and logging requirements are.

Running costs include AI usage (which varies with volume and task complexity), hosting and ongoing evaluation. We estimate these with you before you commit, and set limits so they can't run away.

Questions about custom AI agents

Is an AI agent safe to connect to our systems?

It can be made much safer with limited permissions, approval gates, logs and spending caps, but no agent is risk-free. That's why we start in suggest-only mode and expand access only on evidence. See our security approach.

How is this different from ChatGPT or other AI assistants?

General assistants are great for one-off work a person directs. A custom agent does one defined business task repeatedly, connects to your systems, and follows your rules and approval steps.

What happens when the AI model changes?

Providers update and retire models. We re-run the evaluation suite before switching, so you know whether behavior has changed before it affects your work.
