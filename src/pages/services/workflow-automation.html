What workflow automation is

Every automated workflow has the same shape: a trigger (something happens), a series of steps (read, decide, update, send), and an outcome (a record created, a message sent, a task assigned).

Traditional automation handles steps that follow fixed rules. AI adds the steps that used to need a person to read: pulling details out of a free-text email, sorting a request by type, summarizing a long thread or drafting a first reply. We use AI only for the steps that need it, because rules are cheaper and more predictable.

Good fit

  • A process that happens many times a week and follows a recognizable pattern
  • Staff copying the same details between email, forms, CRM and spreadsheets
  • New inquiries that wait hours for a first reply
  • Clear rules for who handles what, even if they're only in people's heads

Probably not a fit

  • A process that happens a few times a month: the build rarely pays back
  • Every case is different and needs expert judgment from start to finish
  • Your software has no way to connect (no API, export or integration)
  • You're not sure which process to start with: begin with an assessment

Testing & failure handling

Designed for the day something goes wrong

  • We test with realistic sample data, including messy and incomplete cases, before connecting live data
  • AI steps that fall below a confidence threshold go to a person instead of guessing
  • Customer-facing messages, money and commitments need human approval unless you explicitly decide otherwise
  • Failed steps retry where safe, then alert a named person; nothing fails silently
  • Every run is logged so you can see what happened and why

Questions about workflow automation

What's the difference between AI automation and regular automation?

Regular automation follows fixed rules: "when a form is submitted, create a contact." AI automation adds steps that need reading or writing, such as working out what a customer is asking for. We combine both and use AI only where rules can't do the job. More in AI vs. traditional automation.

Will it send messages to customers without anyone checking?

Not by default. Customer-facing messages start with human approval. Once you've seen enough correct drafts, you can choose to let low-risk messages send automatically.

Who owns the automation?

You do. We build in accounts your business controls wherever possible, and document the workflow so another provider or your own staff could maintain it.
