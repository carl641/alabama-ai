Controls

How we work with your systems and data

Least-privilege access

We ask for the narrowest access that lets the work get done: read-only where possible, limited to the specific folders, inboxes, calendars or records the workflow uses. Each person on our side gets their own named login, never a shared one. Access is reviewed during the project and removed at the end unless you've contracted ongoing support.

Client-owned accounts

AI platforms, automation tools and phone numbers are set up in your business's name, billed to you, with you as the owner or administrator. You can see what's running, what it costs, and cut off our access at any time.

Credentials and secrets

We don't ask you to email passwords. Where a system supports it, you invite us as a user or grant access through the vendor's own permission screen. API keys are stored in the platform's secret storage, not in documents or chat messages. {{todo: Name the password manager or secret-handling method actually used and whether multi-factor authentication is enforced on agency accounts.}}

Data minimization

We design workflows to send AI models only the information needed for the task. Sensitive fields that aren't needed are left out or masked. Test and demo work uses sample or de-identified data wherever possible.

Human approval

Actions that commit your business, such as quoting prices, making promises, sending sensitive replies, moving money, or changing records that matter, go to a named person to approve. Escalation paths send urgent or unusual cases straight to your team.

Testing before and after launch

We test with realistic and adversarial cases: wrong or missing information, off-topic requests, attempts to extract information or override instructions, and vendor outages. Known limits are documented. We re-test after significant changes to prompts, workflows or the underlying AI model.

AI model providers

What happens inside the AI service

AI model providers have their own terms on whether they store inputs and whether they use them to improve their models. Those terms differ between consumer products and business or API accounts, and they change over time.

For each project, we choose account types and settings appropriate to the data involved, document them in your scope, and show you where to check them. We recommend reviewing the provider's current terms yourself before go-live.

Incidents

If something goes wrong

If we suspect that client data has been exposed or an account has been misused, we will contain the issue, tell you promptly, explain what we know and don't know, and help you through next steps. Every system we build has a documented way to pause it.
